## 1. Snapshot

**Company.** MiniMax Group, Shanghai, founded December 2021 (Yan Junjie, Yun Yeyi, Zhou Yucong); Alibaba-led $600M round at a $2.5B valuation in March 2024; listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (SEHK: 100) on 2026-01-09. Consumer products: Talkie (character chat), Hailuo AI (video), MiniMax Agent, plus speech/music models [42].

**Other modalities** [1][15][20][23]: video `MiniMax-H3` / `MiniMax-H3-Max` (open weights "MiniMax-H3", 33B, Aug 2026) and `MiniMax-Hailuo-2.3(-Fast)`, `Hailuo-02`; speech `speech-2.8-hd` / `speech-2.8-turbo`, voice clone, voice design, ASR `asr-1.0`; image `image-01`, vision `MiniMax-VL-01`; music `music-3.0` (open weights "MiniMax-Music3", 2B; the music API is restricted for new users from 2026-08-20 [20]).

**Release cadence.** A text model roughly every 2–3 months: M2 (Oct 2025) → M2.1 → M2.5 (Feb 2026) → M2.7 (Mar 2026) → M3 (Jun 2026), with Speech 2.8 (2026-01-23), Music 3.0 (2026-07-16) and H3 (API 2026-07-31; open weights on Hugging Face mid-Aug 2026) as the media releases [23][42][55][57]. Newer-than-this-doc scan (2026-09-24): the model release notes end at H3 (2026-07-31) and the API release notes at 2025-10-28; the HF org, OpenRouter and HN show nothing newer than M3 / H3 / Music 3.0 — no M3.x or M4 found (unverified as of 2026-09-24 beyond vendor pages + HN, since web search was unavailable) [57][23][35][48].

**Positioning.** MiniMax is the "cheap, open-weights, agentic-coding" vendor: M3 ships with a 1M context, native multimodality, Anthropic- and OpenAI-compatible endpoints, pay-as-you-go prices of $0.30/$1.20 per 1M tokens (a "permanent 50% discount" is baked into the list) [1], and a $22–$132/mo Token Plan aimed squarely at Claude Code / Cursor / OpenClaw users [2][3]. Benchmarks put M3 in the "near-frontier at 1/10 the price" band (AA Intelligence Index 29, #16 of 113 [29]; SWE-bench Verified 80.5% self-reported [24]) while human-preference arenas rank it well below the top (Arena text #84 [32]). The company also carries baggage: a Feb 2026 Anthropic allegation of a 13M+-exchange distillation campaign [43] and a Hollywood copyright suit over Hailuo [42].

## 3. Headless / server automation fit

- **Non-interactive use on macOS/Linux.** Three clean paths: (1) plain HTTPS via the `anthropic`/`openai` Python SDKs with `base_url` overridden [7][8]; (2) `mcode exec "<prompt>" --json --auto-approve --model MiniMax-M3` for a full coding-agent run, explicitly billed as "for shell scripts, CI, batch work, and evaluations" [37]; (3) Claude Code itself with `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL=https://api.minimax.io/anthropic` and `ANTHROPIC_AUTH_TOKEN=<minimax key>` (MiniMax's own recommended settings.json) [9].
- **Auth modes.** Pay-as-you-go API key (platform console); Token Plan **Subscription Key** prefixed `sk-cp-…`, "not interchangeable with pay-as-you-go API Keys" [3][13]; OAuth browser login for OpenClaw ("MiniMax Global — OAuth (minimax.io)") and `mcode login --region global` [13][37]. No device-code flow documented.
- **May the subscription be used programmatically?** Yes in practice: the "Other tools" guide gives the Anthropic and OpenAI base URLs for the `sk-cp-…` key and the FAQ says the key works "across all supported tools" with one shared quota [10][4]. The FAQ's stated policy is throttling, not prohibition: "some requests come from ultra-high-concurrency automated batch tasks or multi-user sharing patterns"; "requests may be throttled when exceeded; typically reset within ~1 minute and may tighten during peak traffic" (peak = weekdays 15:00–17:30; timezone confirmed unstated on the FAQ as of 2026-09-24 — not resolvable from vendor pages) [4]. The Default Team may not invite members (personal use only); no refunds [4].
- **ToS / privacy (retrieved 2026-09-24 from the Next.js page data of `platform.minimax.io/protocol/…`; the pages are JS-rendered and return only a title to plain fetches, which is why earlier passes found nothing) [65][66].** Contracting entity: **Nanonoble Pte. Ltd., 152 Beach Road, #14-02 Gateway East, Singapore 189721**; both documents effective **2026-03-30**. Governing law **Singapore**, disputes to **SIAC arbitration** [66]. **Residency:** "Your personal data described in this Policy are stored in the data center located in the United States and processed by us or third-party vendors … including the GDPR" — i.e. the global endpoint's data plane is US-hosted, the operator is a Singapore entity of a Shanghai-listed group [65][42]. **Training use:** the ToS says "We may use the input and generated content to provide, maintain, develop, and improve our Services, comply with applicable law, enforce our terms and policies, and keep our Services safe" — "improve our Services" is the usual training-eligible wording and **no opt-out, ZDR or enterprise-tier data addendum is published**; the privacy policy only promises not to use "personal data for training to profile or target consumers" and reserves the right to "mine, analyze and utilize the database commercially" after de-identification [65][66]. **Retention:** "for as long as it is necessary or permitted by law" — **no numeric window** [65]. **Availability:** "We offer limited service availability guarantees within the scope outlined in the service rules … If your availability requirements exceed those specified in the service rules, you must proactively configure your own systems for high availability" — no numeric SLA anywhere, priority tier included [66][1]. **Regulated data:** US service region forbids ITAR content and HIPAA data [66]. **Tax:** "sales tax or VAT may apply based on your billing location" [66] — a candidate (not a confirmed) explanation for the ~10% $20/$50/$120 vs $22/$55/$132 gap in §4.
- **Serving other people.** The ToS is written for developers who build "Applications" used by "end-users": you are "responsible for all use of the service by end-users", must issue "legally effective privacy notices to end-users", obtain their consent for MiniMax to process "Client Data", and label deep-synthesis output — so **pay-as-you-go API output may be served to non-owner users** (pickem league dashboard, shared project sites) subject to those duties; what is prohibited is to "independently sublicense, resell, or distribute any or all services outside of any integrated applications" [66]. The **Token Plan is the exception**: the FAQ treats "multi-user sharing patterns" as abuse to throttle and the Default Team cannot invite members [4] — route anything a second person consumes through the pay-as-you-go key, not the `sk-cp-` key.
- **Rate limits (pay-as-you-go).** `MiniMax-M3` 200 RPM / 10M TPM; M2.7/M2.5/M2.1/M2 (+highspeed) 500 RPM / 20M TPM; speech 60 RPM; H3 300 RPM / 30 in-flight; image-01 10 RPM; raise via api@minimax.io [15]. Token Plan quotas: 5-hour rolling window + weekly window, sized only as "3–4 / 4–5 / 6–7 agents" per tier; token counts are not published [2][3].
- **Sandboxing.** `mcode` has `--sandbox` for tool execution and a permission-controlled environment; `--yolo` bypasses checks [37]. Server-side: none (no code-interpreter tool) [19].
- **Structured event output for orchestration.** `mcode exec --json` / `--stream-json` [37]; API streaming via SSE on both compat endpoints with usage in the stream [7][8].
- **Session resume.** `mcode --continue` / `mcode --session` [37]. API is stateless; you must replay the full turn history including reasoning blocks [18].
- **Streaming.** Supported on Anthropic-compat (`stream=True`) and OpenAI-compat (`stream` + `stream_options.include_usage`) [7][8].
- **Caching for repeated system prompts.** Automatic caching on M3/M2.7/M2.5/M2.1 (≥512 input tokens, no write charge, TTL "adjusted by system load"); explicit `cache_control` on the Anthropic endpoint for M2.x only (M3 not listed), 5-minute TTL, ≤4 breakpoints, write $0.375/1M [16][17].

## 5. Strengths & weaknesses per reviews

**Benchmarks.**

**Cross-doc dimension gaps — MiniMax rows (gap-fill pass 3, 2026-09-24; fetch-only, search budget exhausted).**

- **Concurrency per lane on subscription auth.** Only the FAQ's tier language exists: Plus "approximately 3–4 agents", Max "4–5", Ultra "6–7", stated for peak hours (weekdays 15:00–17:30, timezone unstated), with throttling that "typically reset[s] within ~1 minute" [4]. There is no hard parallel-session cap on the `sk-cp-` key — quota is token-windowed (5 h rolling + weekly), not slot-limited — so the scheduler's fan-out for this lane should be **rate-driven, not slot-driven**: read `current_interval_remaining_percent` / `current_interval_status` from `/v1/token_plan/remains` before each dispatch and hold new MiniMax jobs when status = 2 (exhausted) or remaining < a floor; start with 3 concurrent on Plus and let the counter, not a fixed slot count, govern [4][75]. Pay-as-you-go is bounded only by 200 RPM / 10M TPM for M3 [15].
- **Credential lifecycle on a headless box.** Three credential shapes: (i) **static `sk-cp-` / `sk-api-` keys** — no expiry, rotation or TTL is documented anywhere on the platform (FAQ, intro, CLI page) [4][3][12]; they live wherever we put them (server env file), never refresh, and fail only on revocation — the right shape for a headless server. (ii) **`mmx` OAuth** — RFC 8628 device-code + PKCE (`POST {host}/oauth2/device/code`, poll every 3 s; the browser open is optional, the user approves the code from any device), credentials persisted **in plaintext** as `oauth: {access_token, refresh_token, expires_at (ISO), region, resource_url, account}` inside `~/.mmx/config.json` (dir mode 0700, `MMX_CONFIG_DIR` override; the `api_key` field lives in the same file; no keychain, no encryption); refresh is silent and browser-free via `POST /oauth2/token grant_type=refresh_token`, triggered when < 5 min of `expires_at` remain, 5xx/timeout retried twice with backoff, **4xx aborts with "run `mmx auth login`"** — that is the first thing to fail after a revoked/expired refresh token, and it needs a human; access-token and refresh-token lifetimes are set server-side (`expired_in`, absolute ms) and are not published [82][83][84][85][86]. (iii) **`mcode` OAuth** — `mcode login --region global`, state under `~/.minimax` (or `~/.minimax-<profile>`, `MINIMAX_DATA_DIR`) alongside `config.yaml`/sessions; "MiniMax OAuth Core manages profile credentials, refresh, and logout" and tool subprocesses get "short-lived access tokens … through a local lease broker"; file names, TTLs and storage format are not documented [37][87][88]. **Expiry alarms:** none built in for any shape; poll `mmx auth status` (OAuth) or watch for HTTP 401 on the key path. Recommendation: use static keys only (§7 step 2), keep `mmx` for `quota` with the key, and avoid the OAuth shapes on the server.
- **Progress-visibility sink.** What this lane can emit: (a) SSE `usage` on both compat endpoints [7][8]; (b) `mcode exec --stream-json` `AgentEvent`s — a 10-value union `agent_start | agent_end | turn_start | turn_end | message_start | message_update | message_end | tool_execution_start | tool_execution_update | tool_execution_end`, carrying `toolCallId / toolName / args / result / isError` but **no usage, cost or session-id fields** [89]; (c) the per-model window counters on `/v1/token_plan/remains` [75]. `mcode`'s own telemetry (three opt-in channels, default off) posts to MiniMax's endpoints (`data.hailuo.ai`, `agent.minimax.io/matrix/api/v1/metrics/batch`, `/observability/desktop-errors/batch`), has **no OTEL/OTLP exporter**, and is silenced by `MCODE_DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1` or `DO_NOT_TRACK=1` — set one of them on the server [90]. For a common cross-lane event schema the mapping is mechanical: `turn_*` → job step, `tool_execution_*` → tool span, `agent_end` → job end; **budget must be inferred from a `remains` poll at job end** (delta of `current_interval_usage_count`), because the event stream carries no tokens. Collector-side evaluation (OTLP/Prometheus/Grafana on the 16 GB Mac) is out of this doc's scope — see the crosscut docs.
- **Empirical calibration.** This doc's cost table (§4) keeps the synthetic 150k-in/15k-out job and a 70% cache-hit assumption; audit-log calibration was outside this task's read scope, so nothing here is measured. Two MiniMax-specific caveats for whoever does the calibration: M3 caching is automatic, ≥512 tokens, TTL "adjusted by system load" and not addressable with `cache_control` [16][17], so the cache-hit share is **not controllable** and must be read back from the usage object per job; and the 350–580 jobs/month break-even (§4) moves to ~230–380 at 0% cache and ~450–750 at 90% — the subscription verdict for this lane is insensitive to the Max-tier question (it is a $22 decision) but sensitive to real jobs/month.
- **Prompt-injection / sandbox posture.** Direct API calls: no server-side code execution, so untrusted Telegram/web/MCP text is only a content risk (no code-interpreter tool exists [19]). `mcode` runs: a layered bash policy (hard-blocked catastrophic patterns such as `rm -rf /`, user deny/ask/allow rules, pipe-to-shell detection, a sensitive-read gate, expedited writes inside the workspace); in **auto** ("YOLO") mode ambiguous commands are routed to a **cloud LLM gate** that sends the tool name, serialized command, a rendering of recent conversation, platform, home directory and workspace root to MiniMax's cloud, where a `gemini-flash` classifier returns allow / confirm / block — fail-closed to "ask the user", which in a headless run means a stall, and it **exfiltrates prompt context (potentially the injected text itself) to a third-party model**; **bypass** mode (`--yolo`) keeps only user-deny and the hard deny list and drops the sensitive-read gate [91][92]. `--sandbox` is a vendored fork of Anthropic's `sandbox-runtime` v0.0.74 (`@minimax/mcode-sandbox-runtime` 0.0.74-mcode.2: deny-first network through a SOCKS5 proxy with a domain allowlist, file-protection scopes independent of the proxy; the upstream uses macOS Seatbelt / Linux bubblewrap) — i.e. the **same OS-sandbox lineage as Claude Code**, so on macOS it inherits the same Seatbelt limits [93][94][95]. A runaway guard flags repeated identical tool calls after ≥3 occurrences and injects a reminder; it does not enforce turn/token/time limits [96]. Cross-lane rank for untrusted inputs: **middle** — OS sandbox + hard deny list is as good as the Claude lane, but the auto-mode cloud gate is a context leak and `--auto-approve` without `--sandbox` is Perplexity/opencode-class. Use `--sandbox --auto-approve`, never `--yolo`, and keep the wall-clock cap.
- **Host resource budget.** Nothing published: `mcode` docs state Node 22.19+/24.2+/25/26 only, no RAM or disk figure [88]; the sandbox adds a Node SOCKS5 proxy process per run [95]. Treat a headless `mcode exec` as one Node process plus a proxy — unmeasured; measure `ps -o rss` on first use and record here. No always-on daemon is required for this lane (everything is per-job).
- **Reviewer independence.** The "different lineage → uncorrelated errors" premise is weakest for exactly this vendor: Anthropic's 2026-02 allegation is that MiniMax's 13M+ distillation exchanges targeted "agentic coding and tool use/orchestration" [43] — the task class we would use M3 to second-review. ## 8. Verdict

1. MiniMax M3 is a credible near-frontier open-weights agentic model at roughly 1/10–1/20 of Claude/GPT per-token prices, with a 1M multimodal context and drop-in Anthropic/OpenAI endpoints [1][7][29][45].
2. It is best used here as a cheap independent reviewer, classifier/router and long-document research worker — not as the orchestrator or sole gate on code [44][46][47].
3. The $22 Token Plan fits the owner's subscription preference, but at our job sizes pay-as-you-go is cheaper below ~350 jobs/month; keep both keys [1][2].
4. Automation fit is strong (headless `mcode exec --json`, streaming SDKs, subscription key usable in scripts) with two caveats: peak-hour throttling of "automated batch" traffic and a noisy September error record [4][37][41].
5. Trust/compliance flags: the Anthropic distillation allegation, training-use-without-opt-out under a Singapore entity with US-hosted data (residency now known, ZDR absent), restrictive weight licences and recent product churn (Teams plan withdrawn) argue for keeping MiniMax on non-sensitive, easily-swappable task classes [43][25][27][6].

Fit scores (1–10): research **7**; coding/agentic **7**; cost efficiency **9**; automation friendliness **7**; trading research **5**.
